# Mental Wellness Platform Design Guidelines

## Design Approach
**Reference-Based Approach** - Drawing inspiration from mental health leaders like Headspace, Calm, and BetterHelp, combined with youth-oriented platforms like Discord for familiar, non-intimidating interfaces.

## Core Design Elements

### Color Palette
**Primary Colors:**
- Light mode: 210 60% 20% (deep calming blue)
- Dark mode: 210 40% 85% (soft light blue)

**Secondary/Accent:**
- Warm support: 25 80% 65% (gentle coral for encouragement)
- Success states: 140 50% 50% (muted green)
- Warning/crisis: 15 90% 60% (warm orange, not alarming red)

**Background Treatments:**
- Subtle gradients from primary to slightly lighter variants
- Soft, organic shapes as background elements
- Never harsh contrasts - prioritize emotional comfort

### Typography
- **Primary:** Inter or Poppins via Google Fonts (friendly, approachable)
- **Sizes:** Large, readable text (18px+ for body) accommodating mobile usage
- **Weights:** Regular (400) and Medium (500) primarily

### Layout System
**Tailwind Spacing:** Consistent use of 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 units
- Generous padding and margins for breathing room
- Mobile-first responsive approach
- Card-based layouts for conversation bubbles and resources

### Component Library

**Chat Interface:**
- Rounded conversation bubbles with generous padding
- User messages: align right with primary color background
- AI responses: align left with subtle background tint
- Typing indicators and smooth message animations

**Navigation:**
- Bottom tab navigation for mobile (Chat, Resources, Help)
- Minimal top navigation with platform logo and crisis button
- Floating action button for new conversations

**Cards & Content:**
- Resource cards with soft shadows and rounded corners
- Crisis help cards with warm (not alarming) highlighting
- Progress indicators using gentle animations

**Forms & Inputs:**
- Large, touch-friendly input fields
- Soft focus states without harsh outlines
- Anonymous login options prominently displayed

## Visual Treatment

**Cultural Sensitivity:**
- Avoid overly clinical or medical aesthetics
- Use inclusive imagery representing diverse Indian youth
- Incorporate subtle patterns inspired by Indian textiles (very subtle)

**Emotional Design:**
- Prioritize feelings of safety and welcome over efficiency
- Use micro-interactions that feel supportive (gentle haptics, smooth transitions)
- Color temperature should feel warm and inviting, never cold

## Images
**Hero Section:** Medium-sized hero (not full viewport) featuring diverse young people in supportive, non-clinical settings - perhaps students studying together or friends having coffee. Should feel aspirational but relatable.

**Resource Cards:** Small supportive illustrations rather than stock photos - simple line art or gentle illustrations representing coping strategies.

**Background Elements:** Abstract, organic shapes and gradients rather than photography to maintain focus on conversations and content.
